# Retrospective evaluation method

Protocol `carer-national-v1` fixes development data to January 2012–September 2024 and reserves October 2024–September 2026 from model selection. The source is the frozen national DSS release described in [DATA](DATA.md). This protocol is committed before computing predictive errors. The final period was already published at implementation time; it is an untouched computational holdout, not a prospective study.

Expanding development windows begin with 60 training months. Each origin predicts the next 1, 2 and 3 months using only the prefix available at that origin. There are 91 development origins, each with three targets wholly before October 2024. No random split, future covariate or full-series preprocessing is used.

The fixed candidates are last value, seasonal naive (same month a year earlier), additive damped Holt exponential smoothing without a seasonal component, and a direct ridge model predicting the change from the last observation. Ridge uses alpha 10, a scaler fitted within the training window and lagged monthly changes, trailing means and known calendar seasonality. There is no hyperparameter search. Damped Holt is scaled by its training mean for numerical conditioning; parameters are estimated within the training window. Non-finite output or unsuccessful optimisation is an explicit error, not a silently successful forecast. Point forecasts are clipped at zero.

The candidate with the lowest pooled development MAE is selected, with ties resolved in the order above. All four candidates remain visible for comparison. The choice is locked before evaluating the holdout. Models refit on growing prefixes during holdout evaluation, incorporating earlier holdout observations only after their dates have passed. No holdout errors are used for reselection or calibration. The 22 common origins produce 22 predictions per horizon: horizon 1 covers October 2024–July 2026, horizon 2 November 2024–August 2026, and horizon 3 December 2024–September 2026. These overlapping targets are dependent, not 66 independent experiments.

Errors are prediction minus published rounded observation. Metrics are MAE, RMSE, signed bias, fraction underpredicted and seasonal MASE. Each MASE error uses the mean absolute lag-12 difference in its own training prefix; a zero scale gives null, never division by zero. No demographic or regional fairness result is possible from this national-only projection.

For the selected candidate, symmetric nominal 80% and 95% intervals use horizon-specific absolute development errors, with order rank `ceil((n+1)*nominal)`. Widths remain fixed throughout the holdout. Lower bounds are clipped at zero. Calibration residuals overlap in time, can include policy shocks and were also used for model selection. These intervals carry no exchangeability or coverage guarantee; empirical holdout coverage and width must be reported. Point forecasts and bounds are estimates on a rounded target, not reconstructed individual counts.

A series-end extrapolation is measured from the last reporting month, September 2026. Publication and acquisition occur later; an October target can already be underway when the September release is available. This is not a live operational forecast. Policy changes, including the March 2020 transfer noted by DSS, prevent treating patterns as causal changes in caring need.

Implementation references: [statsmodels exponential smoothing](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.tsa.holtwinters.ExponentialSmoothing.html), [scikit-learn Ridge](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.linear_model.Ridge.html), and [train-only scaling guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html). Dependencies are pinned for reproducibility; installed versions are recorded with results.
